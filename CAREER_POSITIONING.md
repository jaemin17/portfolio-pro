# Career Positioning Context

## Core Positioning

I am not trying to package myself as a strong visual-only designer, and I am
not positioning myself as a pure B2B tools designer. I want to become an App
product experience designer whose work feels clear, calm, detailed, and
thoughtful.

## Background

I studied industrial design at China Academy of Art and later moved into UI/UX.
My early work included mobile internet product optimization, websites,
automotive interfaces, and home appliance websites. Later, I worked more on
VR/XR, 3D education software, resource platforms, admin systems, tool websites,
3D editors, branding, and official websites.

My experience is closer to real product delivery and independent execution than
to a typical large-company, process-heavy design path.

## Strengths

- Clear, calm, and detailed product experiences
- Interaction thinking, information structure, and interface states
- Functional apps, tools, AI products, productivity products, journaling,
  learning, and self-management products
- Clean, gentle, restrained visual taste
- Turning messy product needs into usable, comfortable interfaces

## Less Suitable Directions

- Strong visual-impact marketing design
- Operation banners and campaign visuals
- Illustration-heavy UI
- Game UI
- Pure graphic design or brand-material production
- Purely cold B2B admin systems with little emotional or experiential detail

## Gaps To Express Better

I have less mature-team and formal-process presentation experience. In my
portfolio, research, competitive analysis, process, design systems, outcomes,
and retrospectives need to be expressed more clearly.

This does not mean I am not professional. It means many professional judgments
from past work were not previously organized into visible evidence.

## Job Direction

I am more interested in App product experience design, especially work that
combines logic, emotional detail, and small creative decisions. I am not aiming
for pure B2B, pure visual design, or purely operational design.

An ideal team would be medium-sized, have an existing design process, include a
design lead or senior designers, and still allow me to own clear modules and
contribute ideas.

## Portfolio Goal

The first impression should be: gentle, comfortable, and tasteful.

The deeper impression should be: professional, smart, and judgment-driven.

The portfolio should not become flashy. It should strengthen evidence around:

- Problem definition
- Design decisions
- User flows and structure
- Design systems or reusable patterns
- Before-and-after comparisons
- Implementation and collaboration
- Why a design direction was chosen

## Communication Preference

Please stay neutral and professional. Do not blindly agree with me. Keep answers
short and focused, and ask active questions that help surface the real problem.

My thinking may be scattered. Help decide which information matters, which is
emotion, and which is noise.

## Design Critique Preference

I am sensitive to vague criticism of taste or visual ability. If evaluating my
design, be specific. Explain whether the issue is about hierarchy, layout,
style, commercial feeling, polish, interaction logic, information architecture,
or another concrete dimension.

Avoid vague comments such as "ugly", "not premium", or "not professional"
without explaining the underlying design reason.
